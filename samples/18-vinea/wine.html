Tasting notes · by Hélène Vauquelin

Velvet, then smoke.

A warm, dry summer gave small berries with thick skins. We picked over four mornings in mid-September and let the wine rest eighteen months in our own oak.

I.

The eye

Deep garnet with a violet rim; slow, glossy legs that cling to the glass.

II.

The nose

Black cherry and crushed violet first, then cedar, clove and a cool hint of forest floor after rain.

III.

The palate

Silky and long. Ripe, fine-grained tannins, bright acidity and a finish of dark chocolate and smoke.

How it was made

Four mornings, by hand.

The Clos is a walled 2.1-hectare parcel on pure limestone, facing south-east at 290 metres. Its vines were planted by Hélène’s grandfather in 1961.
